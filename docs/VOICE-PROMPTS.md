# Voice prompts and tones (Turkish)

The toy talks to the child at the moments where something has to happen or has gone wrong.
Prompts are MP3 files on the SD card, generated by `tools/sounds/gen_prompts.py` (macOS `say`,
voice Yelda) into `sdcard/sounds/`, and played by `VoicePrompts` (src/VoicePrompts.*).

## Card layout

```
/sounds/tr/<name>.mp3     speech, Turkish
/sounds/tr/prompts.json   the texts (reference, version)
/sounds/tone_*.mp3        tones
/sounds/beep.mp3          volume beep
```

Write a card with `tools/sdcard-prepare.sh /dev/diskN` (erases it, FAT32, copies `sdcard/`).
A card without the files works: the toy stays silent and logs `prompts: cannot play`.
Until the factory writes the cards, a missing pack could also be fetched from the portal
(`/sounds/<lang>/` manifest) on the first connection; not built yet.

## Playback rules

- One prompt at a time, queued (8 deep), the same prompt never twice within its interval.
- A prompt marked *interrupt* pauses a playing story, plays, and the story resumes at the
  same position (the resumed story stays silent until the seek has landed). Everything else
  is dropped while a story plays (the story wins). There is deliberately no "wait for
  silence" policy: no prompt needed it, and a prompt spoken minutes after its cause (a story
  can run for 20 minutes) only confuses; the dead branch and its 30 s timeout were removed
  in 1.2.18.
- `say()` may be called from any task (the provisioning prompts come from the WiFi event
  task): the "already queued" bit is claimed atomically, the loop drains the queue.
- The volume beep (`/sounds/beep.mp3`, `VoicePrompts::kBeepPath`) goes through the same path
  as a prompt: it never advances the playlist or re-announces a download wait when it ends.
- `sayBlocking` is used only where the device is about to go away (sleep, WiFi reset,
  battery cutoff).
- Prompts do not change the LED scene (the "playing" scene is for stories).

## Remote update: prompt packs

Since firmware 1.2.15 the sound files are a *prompt pack* that the portal can replace
remotely, paired with firmware releases (portal docs/OTA.md "Voice prompt packs").

- **Identity.** `sounds/pack.json` carries the pack checksum: sha256 of the sorted
  `path:sha256` lines of every mp3 under `sounds/` (json files and dotfiles excluded), joined
  with `\n` plus a trailing `\n`. `tools/sounds/gen_prompts.py` writes it and also produces
  `releases/prompts-<id>.zip`, the file to upload on the portal's Firmware page ("Voice prompt
  packs"). `tools/sdcard-prepare.sh` copies `pack.json` to the card, so a factory card reports
  the pack it carries. The same zip serves as the flasher page's `sounds.zip`. Packs made or
  edited on the portal (own recordings instead of TTS) are downloaded from the Firmware page
  and written with `tools/sdcard-prepare.sh /dev/diskN prompts-<id>.zip`.
- **No ambiguity about the files.** The firmware embeds the exact list it expects
  (`TTPROMPTS1:tr/setup_welcome.mp3,...,beep.mp3`, generated from the prompt list in
  VoicePrompts.h). The portal reads it from the uploaded `.bin` and only lets a pack be assigned
  to a release when it contains every file; on upload it auto-assigns the newest pack that does.
- **How the device learns.** Every firmware check (`/firmware/check?prompts=1&prompt_pack=<card
  checksum>`) answers with the pack paired with the offered release, or with the running one
  when nothing is to install. The device reports the card's pack and a state
  (`ok / outdated / syncing / missing / unknown`) in its device report; the hub page shows it.
- **Update flow (PromptPackManager + OtaManager).** When a release is offered whose pack differs
  from the card: "Kendimi güncelliyorum…" (from the old pack) → the pack is downloaded into
  `/sounds.staging` (sha256 per file, OTA state `prompts`) → the image downloads → reboot → the
  new firmware finds `/sounds.staging/pack.json` with `for_firmware` equal to its own version
  and swaps it in before its first word. `/sounds` is never touched by the old firmware, so a
  failed or interrupted image download leaves old prompts with old firmware; the staging folder
  is reused on the next attempt and dropped when no update is on the way any more.
- **Rollback.** The new firmware keeps the previous pack as `/sounds.old` until the image is
  confirmed (portal reached, or 10 min of uptime; docs/OTA-STATE-MACHINE.md). If the bootloader rolls back, the previous firmware finds a pack marked for
  another version next to `/sounds.old` and restores its own.
- **Pack changed for the current release / card without a pack.** The device downloads it the
  same way and swaps it in as soon as no prompt is playing.
- **Transport.** Pack files are fetched like story tracks, over plain HTTP on port 80 (sha256
  verified); nginx must serve `/storage/prompts/` on port 80 without the HTTPS redirect, as it
  does for `/storage/tracks/`. A redirect shows up as the pack's files staying "queued".
- **If the pack cannot be downloaded** twice in a row, the image is installed anyway and the new
  firmware fetches its pack at its first check, so a broken pack never blocks a firmware update.

## Changing a text

Edit `PROMPTS` in `tools/sounds/gen_prompts.py`, run it, upload `releases/prompts-<id>.zip` on
the Firmware page and assign it to the release(s); devices pick it up at their next check.
A new prompt also needs an entry in the list in `src/VoicePrompts.h` (enum, file name) and a
policy row in `VoicePrompts.cpp`; the embedded schema then requires it, and the portal refuses
packs that lack the file.

## Faults and the voice (2026-10-29)
A fault's prompt is spoken whenever the fault is raised with `speak` (the default), subject
only to the prompt's own minimum interval above; the 10-minute dedupe of the fault log never
mutes it (it used to: a second empty figure or a second wrong password within 10 minutes was
silent, and `download_failed` was never heard because a silent track-level raise always came
first). `battery_low` (501) and `nfc_read_trouble` (702) are reachable again. An update that
was only postponed (toy unplugged or under 15 %) is logged, not spoken as "update failed".
