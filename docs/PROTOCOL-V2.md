# Protocol v2: identity, pairing, entitlement, updates, resets (decisions of 2026-10-23)

Owner decisions of 2026-10-23 turned into one contract both repos
implement. FW = firmware, P = portal. Everything here replaces earlier docs where they differ.

## 1. Identity and enrollment

- The device id stays the chip MAC in decimal (`uuid`). It is public; nothing is derived from
  it alone any more.
- At the factory every unit gets **two secrets**, generated by the portal when the unit is
  enrolled and written into the device's NVS `factory` namespace over USB:
  - `secret`: 32 random bytes, base64url (43 chars). Only the portal (hashed) and the device
    know it. Used to obtain the device token.
  - `pop` (proof of possession / pairing code): 8 characters from `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789`
    shown as `TK-XXXX-XXXX`, printed on the label as text and inside the QR, and used as the
    BLE proof of possession **exactly in that printed form** (`TK-XXXX-XXXX`, upper case, with
    the dashes); the portal's bind endpoint accepts either spelling. It is also used as the
    BLE provisioning proof-of-possession and as the pairing code the app sends when binding.
- P: `POST /api/factory/hubs` (admin session or an admin token; the manufacturing panel):
  `{uuid, device_id, hardware_rev, serial?}` → creates or re-enrolls the hub, stores
  `secret_hash`, `pop_hash`, `serial`, `enrolled_at`, returns `{uuid, secret, pop, serial}`
  **once**. Re-enrolling rotates both (an old label stops working).
- FW: `ft enroll <secret> <pop> [serial]` stores them; `ft show` prints uuid, MAC (correct byte
  order), serial, enrolled yes/no (never the secret). The BLE PoP is the stored `pop`; the
  hard-coded `abcd1234` disappears (a device without a pop cannot be provisioned over BLE and
  says so with the 112 NOT_ENROLLED fault / red LED).
- Un-enrolled units (development boards, bare flashes) pair with the shared development PIN,
  by design and permanently: this is how a development device stays outside the fleet. The
  printed `TK-XXXX-XXXX` code applies only once the factory line has enrolled the unit. The
  factory identity lives in the `factory` NVS namespace and survives OTA updates and customer
  resets; only a full flash erase (the station's Flash step) or `ft reset --all` removes it,
  after which the station re-enrolls the unit. Fault 112 NOT_ENROLLED (info) marks such units
  on the hub page.
- Label QR payload: `TT1:<uuid>:<pop>` (the app parses it; the human-readable pop sits under
  the QR).

## 2. Device token

- FW: `POST /api/hubs/{uuid}/token` with header `X-Hub-Secret: <secret>`; no body. P verifies
  the hash and returns `{token, expires_in}` (Sanctum token **on the Hub model**, name
  `device`, abilities `["device"]`, expires in 90 days; issuing a new one revokes the hub's
  previous device tokens). The device stores it in NVS and refreshes on 401, on 403 from a
  hub endpoint (once per 10 min), and 7 days before expiry.
- Answers: 404 = hub not enrolled (FW raises 112 NOT_ENROLLED, info, red blink while
  provisioning is impossible), 401 = wrong secret (112 as well, detail `secret`),
  429 = throttled (back off).
- The legacy `GET /api/hubs/{uuid}/token` (owner's token) stays only while
  `config('tilkie.legacy_device_token')` is true and the hub has no `secret_hash`; it is turned
  off once the three existing hubs are enrolled.
- A device token is a **device principal** (`HybridAuth` sets `request->attributes['hub']`;
  `auth()->user()` is null). It may call: the token endpoint, `/units/{nfc}` (manifest),
  `/hubs/{uuid}/firmware/*`, `/hubs/{uuid}/events`, `/broadcasting/auth` for
  `private-device.{its uuid}` only. Everything user-facing rejects it with 403.
- A device gets its token whether or not it is bound to a user: firmware checks, fault events
  and the prompt pack work from the factory on.

## 3. Pairing (binding a hub to a user)

- App: `POST /api/hubs/{uuid}/bind` with `{pairing_code}` (the pop). Possession wins: with a
  valid code the hub is bound to the caller even if it was bound to someone else (resale);
  the previous owner is detached and notified later through the app. Without the code: 403.
  Admin binding (portal UI) needs no code. 5 wrong codes per hub per hour → 429.
- Unbind (app or admin): detaches the user. The device keeps its own token; the device's
  channel stays owner-only, so the old app loses it. Nothing else changes on the device.
- Not bound: the device works (plays any card, updates, reports); the app cannot command it.
  FW raises 110 NOT_LINKED (info, fruit **kivi**) when it is online and `GET /api/hubs/{uuid}`
  (new, device principal) answers `bound: false`; the prompt `not_linked` is spoken once per
  boot after the idle hello and once a day after that: "Henüz bir hesaba bağlı değilim. Beni
  uygulamadan ekle."

## 4. Cards and entitlement

- Any toy may play any card (default and wildcard); the manifest stays open to device
  principals and signed-in users.
- A wildcard card is **modifiable only by its holder**: content bind/unbind and custom-track
  bind/unbind on a unit require the caller to own the unit (API and admin alike).
- Unbinding a wildcard card from a user detaches that user's contents and custom tracks from
  the card and syncs the hubs of everyone who had it cached (DeviceContentSync).
- A deleted card answers `410 {"revoked": true}` to the manifest request; FW purges its cache
  on 410 (not on 404 or network errors).

## 5. Updates (dock only; the state machine)

States: IDLE, CHECKING, WAITING, PROMPTS, DOWNLOADING, APPLYING, REBOOTING, FAILED.

- **Checks**: 90–120 s after boot, hourly, on the portal's nudge, and **when the toy is
  docked** (VBUS appears) if the last check is older than 10 min.
- **Offer**: accepted when `version_code > running`, or explicit (pin or command) and not in
  the device's rolled-back list. The offer lives in RAM with its time.
- **WAITING** ends when all hold: VBUS present, nothing playing or paused, no figure on the
  pins, no story download running. A **mandatory** release drops the figure/playing
  conditions after 1 h of waiting but still needs the dock. While WAITING the toy may sleep on
  battery; the boot check re-offers.
- Before leaving WAITING, if the offer is older than 10 min the device re-checks (fresh URL,
  release may have changed or been revoked); the re-check's answer replaces the offer.
- **PROMPTS** stages the pack paired with the release (unchanged pack → skipped).
- **DOWNLOADING**: undocking does not abort (the download is minutes); only battery under 15 %
  aborts to FAILED. A 403/410 on the download re-checks once instead of failing.
- **APPLYING → REBOOTING**; the new image is confirmed when the portal has been reached
  (Reverb connected or a 200 from the API) or after 10 min of uptime, whichever first.
- **Rollback**: the device stores the rolled-back release id in NVS and declines it in later
  offers (posts status `declined`); P never re-offers automatically a release whose last event
  for that hub is `rolled_back`; a new pin or command overrides both.
- **version_code** is derived from the image by P only (no manual field); FW's formula:
  exactly `major.minor.patch`, each < 100, `v` prefix refused at build time.
- FAILED returns to IDLE after 10 min. Signed URL TTL on P: 6 hours.

## 6. WiFi: open networks and captive portals

- Open networks are accepted (empty password stored and mirrored). After every connection the
  device probes `GET http://<host of the configured API base>/storage/ping.json` (plain HTTP,
  port 80 for an https base, the base's own port for an http bench base; no redirects
  followed, 5 s). Three outcomes:
  - **online**: the body is exactly `{"ping":"tilkie"}` after trimming whitespace;
  - **captive portal**: an answer arrives but is not ours (any 3xx, HTML, another body, not
    HTTP; a DNS hijack shows up as one of these);
  - **no internet**: no answer at all (DNS failure, connect timeout, reset, silence for 5 s).
  Network services (token, Reverb, checks, downloads) start only once the probe says online;
  captive and no internet are both "offline" with a re-probe every 2 minutes.
- During provisioning the probe runs before the provisioning manager reports success. Only a
  captive portal is rejected: the app sees a failed connection (the same status as a wrong
  password: FAIL / auth error), the credentials are forgotten, 113 and `setup_captive`. A
  network without internet (or a portal outage) is accepted as before: the app sees success,
  the credentials stay, the toy waits offline and comes online when the probe passes (106
  PORTAL_UNREACHABLE, silent, once per link).
- During provisioning a captive network is **rejected**: the credentials are forgotten, fault
  113 CAPTIVE_PORTAL (warning, fruit **nar**) is raised, the prompt `setup_captive` is spoken
  ("Bu ağ bir giriş sayfası istiyor, lütfen başka bir ağ seç. Hata kodu: Nar.") and
  provisioning restarts. An already provisioned toy that lands on a captive network behaves as
  offline (no prompt spam) and re-probes every 2 minutes.

## 7. Resets

- **Customer reset** (buttons 1+4 held 10 s, or the app's `factory-reset` command): forget
  WiFi, delete the device token, wipe the card cache (manifests, assets, queue), clear the
  fault ring and volume settings, ask the portal to unbind (`POST /api/hubs/{uuid}/unbind`
  with the device principal), restart into provisioning. The `factory` namespace (secret,
  pop, serial, bring-up record) survives.
- **Service reset** (`ft reset` over USB): everything the customer reset does plus bring-up
  re-run; the `factory` namespace survives unless `ft reset --all`.
- **Unbound** (the owner removed the toy in the app, an admin unbound the last account, or
  another account claimed it with the pairing code): the portal sends the device command
  `unbound` (value `rebound` when another account took over); a toy that was offline notices
  at its next hub check (`bound: false` after it had been bound, remembered in NVS). The toy
  deletes the custom tracks (`/assets/custom_tracks`) and drops them from its cached figure
  manifests; portal stories, the WiFi credentials and the device token stay. Not a reset: the
  same family can add the toy again without re-pairing.
- Buttons 1+4 held 5 s keeps today's meaning (forget WiFi only).

## 8. Audio formats

- The portal stores MP3 only, mono, 44.1 kHz, 96 kbps for stories and custom tracks (64 kbps
  for prompt packs). Every upload (admin or app) is normalised server-side with ffmpeg in a
  queued job; a track is `ready` only after that and manifests include ready tracks only.
  Checksums are always present; the firmware refuses assets without one.
